# Old Process: Paper-Based Expense Reimbursement

## Overview
Employees who spend personal money on business travel or supplies are reimbursed
through a paper claim that moves between departments by interoffice mail. The
process is owned by Accounts Payable (AP) and has not changed since 2014.

## Steps
1. The employee completes a paper Expense Claim Form (Form EX-12) by hand and
   staples the original receipts to the back of the form.
2. The employee's line manager reviews the claim and signs the form. Claims over
   $500 also need a second signature from the department head.
3. The employee sends the signed form to Accounts Payable by interoffice mail.
   Claims must arrive by the 25th of the month to be included in the next cycle.
4. An AP clerk checks the arithmetic on the form and keys each line into the
   general ledger by hand. Mileage is calculated manually at $0.45 per mile using
   the odometer readings written on the form.
5. Foreign currency expenses are converted at the exchange rate on the day the
   AP clerk keys the claim into the ledger, not the day the money was spent.
6. Reimbursement is paid through the monthly payroll run on the 5th of the
   following month, so employees usually wait 30 to 40 days to be repaid.
7. If a receipt is lost, the employee must sign a written affidavit and the
   department head must countersign it before AP will process the line.

## Controls
- Finance reviews a random 5% sample of claims once per quarter.
- There is no automated check for duplicate claims. Duplicates are caught only
  when a clerk happens to remember a previous submission.
- Policy limits for meals and hotels are printed on the back of Form EX-12 and
  are enforced only if the manager notices a breach.

## Known Issues
- About 12% of claims are returned to the employee for arithmetic errors or
  missing signatures, which adds another full cycle to the wait.
- Employees have no way to see where their claim is. They email AP to ask.
- Paper forms and receipts are stored in boxes for seven years, and retrieval
  for an audit can take several days.
- Claims from staff in remote offices often miss the 25th deadline because
  interoffice mail takes up to a week to arrive.
