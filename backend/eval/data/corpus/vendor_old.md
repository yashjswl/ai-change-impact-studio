# Old Process: Email-Driven Vendor Onboarding

## Overview
New suppliers are added through a chain of emails between the requesting team,
Procurement and Finance. The vendor list lives in a shared Excel workbook called
Vendor Master, and there is no single system that records the state of a request.

## Steps
1. The requesting team emails Procurement with the vendor name, contact and the
   goods or services needed.
2. A procurement officer replies with a Word vendor application form. The vendor
   fills it in and returns it by email together with a W-9 tax form and a bank
   letter.
3. The procurement officer checks the vendor against the sanctions list by
   searching the OFAC website by hand and saves a screenshot of the result.
4. Bank details are verified by phoning the bank using the number printed on the
   bank letter.
5. The officer adds a row to the Vendor Master workbook and sends it to Finance
   for approval by email.
6. Finance creates the vendor record in the ERP system after receiving the
   approved row. This step is often delayed until the next weekly batch.
7. Signed contracts are printed and kept in a physical binder in the Procurement
   office.

## Risk Assessment
There is no risk assessment for vendors with annual spend under $25,000. Larger
vendors receive a review that depends on which procurement officer picks up the
request, so the depth of review varies from one vendor to another.

## Known Issues
- Onboarding takes 15 to 20 business days on average.
- An internal audit found about 140 duplicate vendor records, mostly the same
  company entered twice under slightly different names.
- Vendors are never re-validated after onboarding. Sanctions status and bank
  details can go years without a check.
- No one is warned when a contract is close to expiry, so some contracts lapse
  while the vendor continues to be paid.
- Requesting teams have no way to see progress and call Procurement for updates.
